(i) One or more recyclable materials, as determined by the county's material management plan, that are typically collected through curbside recycling programs, are collected at least twice per month.

(i) In counties with a population of less than 100,000, there is at least 1 drop-off location for each 10,000 residents without access to curbside recycling at their the residents' dwelling, and the drop-off location is available at least not less than 24 hours per month.

(c) Lateral expansions of coal combustion residuals landfills or coal combustion residuals impoundments that engage in solid waste management of coal combustion residuals generated from the combustion of coal at an electric utility or independent power producer.

(d) Unless otherwise provided in this subpart, off-site disposal areas of an electric utility or independent power producer. This subpart also applies to any practice that is not a beneficial use of coal combustion residuals.

(e) Inactive coal combustion residuals impoundments at active electric utilities and independent power producers, regardless of how electricity is currently being generated at the facility.

(f) Coal combustion residuals management units at active facilities or facilities with a legacy coal combustion residuals impoundment.

(g) Electric utilities and independent power producers that ceased producing electricity before October 19, 2015 and have a legacy coal combustion residuals impoundment on-site.

(2) This subpart does not apply to any of the following:

(a) Waste, including, but not limited to, fly ash, bottom ash, boiler slag, and flue gas desulfurization materials generated at facilities that are not part of an electric utility or an independent power producer, such as a manufacturing facility, university, or hospital.

(b) Waste, including, but not limited to, fly ash, bottom ash, boiler slag, and flue gas desulfurization materials generated primarily from the combustion of fuels, including, but not limited to, fossil fuels, other than coal, for the purpose of generating electricity, unless the fuel burned consists of more than 50% coal on a total heat input or mass input basis, whichever results in the greater mass feed rate of coal.

(c) Any beneficial use of coal combustion residuals.

(d) Coal combustion residuals placement at active or abandoned underground or surface coal mines.

(e) Municipal solid waste landfills that accept coal combustion residuals.

Sec. 11592. (1) A person shall not establish a coal combustion residuals landfill or coal combustion residuals impoundment except as authorized by a construction permit issued by the department under part 13. A person proposing the establishment of a coal combustion residuals landfill or surface impoundment shall submit an application for a construction permit to the department.

(2) An application for a construction permit for a coal combustion residuals landfill or coal combustion residuals impoundment must be accompanied by all of the following:

(a) Engineering plans.

(b) An application fee in the following amount:

(i) For a coal combustion residuals landfill, $1,000.00.

(ii) For a coal combustion residuals impoundment, $1,000.00.

(iii) For a lateral or vertical expansion of a coal combustion residuals impoundment, $750.00.

(3) If the department returns an application to the applicant as administratively incomplete, the applicant may, not later than 1 year after the application is returned, resubmit the application with the additional information needed to address the reasons for being incomplete, without paying an additional application fee.

(4) If the department denies a permit or an applicant withdraws an application, not later than 1 year after the permit denial or application withdrawal, the applicant may resubmit the application with the additional information needed to address the reasons for the denial or withdrawal, without paying an additional application fee.

(5) Subject to section 11510(2)(d), an application for a modification to a construction permit or for renewal of a construction permit that has expired must be accompanied by a fee of $500.00.

(6) A person may apply for a single permit to construct more than 1 type of disposal area at the same facility. A person that applies to permit more than 1 type of disposal area at the same facility shall pay a fee equal to the sum of the applicable fees listed in this section for each type of disposal area.

(7) The department shall deposit permit application fees collected under this section in the solid waste staff account of the solid waste management fund.

Sec. 11594. (1) Before submitting a construction permit application under section 11592 for a new coal combustion residuals disposal area, a person shall request the department to provide an advisory analysis of the proposed coal combustion residuals disposal area. Not less than 15 days after the request, and notwithstanding an advisory analysis result, the person may file an application for a construction permit. If a construction permit application is not filed within 2 years of issuance of the advisory analysis, a new advisory analysis is required.

(2) On receipt of a construction permit application, the department shall do all of the following:

(a) Immediately notify all of the following:

(i) The clerk of the municipality in which the proposed disposal area is located.

(ii) The local soil erosion and sedimentation control agency under part 93.

(iii) Each division in the department and the department of natural resources that has responsibilities regarding the management of land, air, or water.

(iv) The regional planning agency.

(v) The designated planning agency for the planning area.

(b) Publish a notice in a newspaper or by electronic media with significant circulation or viewership in the vicinity of the proposed disposal area. The notice must contain all of the following:

(i) A map indicating the location of the proposed disposal area.

(ii) A description of the proposed disposal area.

(iii) The location where an individual may review or obtain copies of the complete application.

(iv) An indication that the department will hold a public hearing in the area of the proposed coal combustion residuals disposal area if an applicant, a municipality, or a designated planning agency submits a written request not later than 30 days after the date of publication of the notice, or by a petition submitted to the department containing a number of signatures equal to not less than 10% of the number of registered voters who voted in the last gubernatorial election of the municipality where the proposed disposal area is to be located. The petition must be validated by the clerk of the municipality.

(c) Hold a public hearing after the department preliminarily reviews the application regarding the proposed disposal area and all pertinent data and before a construction permit is issued or denied.

(d) Conduct a consistency review of the proposed disposal area, including, but not limited to, the application regarding the proposed disposal area to determine if the proposed disposal area complies with part 115. The review must be conducted by individuals qualified in hydrogeology and, if the proposed disposal area is a landfill, landfill engineering. The department shall not issue a construction permit unless the individuals conducting the review acknowledge that the application regarding the proposed disposal area complies with the requirements of part 115. An expansion of a disposal area, an enlargement in the capacity of a disposal area, a change in the solid waste boundary or a disposal area, or an alteration of a coal combustion residuals disposal area to a different type of coal combustion residuals disposal area than had been specified in a previous construction permit application constitutes a new proposal for which a new construction permit, rather than a modification of a construction permit, is required. The upgrading of a coal combustion residuals disposal area type required by the department to comply with part 115 or to comply with a consent order does not require a new construction permit.

(e) Notify the Michigan aeronautics commission if the proposed disposal area is a coal combustion residuals landfill or coal combustion residuals impoundment that is a new site or a lateral expansion or vertical expansion of an existing unit proposed to be located not less than 5 miles from a runway or a proposed runway extension contained in a plan approved by the Michigan aeronautics commission of an airport licensed and regulated by the Michigan aeronautics commission. The department shall make a copy of the application regarding the proposed disposal area available to the Michigan aeronautics commission. If, not more than 60 days after receiving notification from the department, the Michigan aeronautics commission informs the department that operation of the proposed disposal area would present a potential hazard to air navigation along with the basis for its findings, the department may either recommend appropriate changes to the location, construction, or operation of the proposed disposal area or deny the application regarding the proposed disposal area. The department shall give an applicant an opportunity to rebut a finding of the Michigan aeronautics commission that the operation of a proposed disposal area would present a potential hazard to air navigation.

(3) The Michigan aeronautics commission shall notify the department and the owner or operator of a coal combustion residuals landfill or coal combustion residuals impoundment if the Michigan aeronautics commission is considering approving a plan that would provide for a runway or the extension of a runway within 5 miles of the coal combustion residuals landfill or coal combustion residuals impoundment.

(4) Before making a final decision regarding a new coal combustion residuals construction permit, the department shall publish a notice in a newspaper or by electronic media having significant circulation in the vicinity of the proposed disposal area. The notice must contain all of the following:

(a) A map indicating the location of the proposed disposal area.

(b) A description of the proposed disposal area.

(c) A description of the department's draft decision and the location where an individual may review or obtain copies of the draft decision.

(d) An indication that the department will hold a public hearing in the area of the proposed coal combustion residuals disposal area if an applicant, a municipality, or a designated planning agency submits a written request not later than 30 days after the date of publication of the notice, or by a petition submitted to the department containing a number of signatures equal to not less than 10% of the number of registered voters who voted in the last gubernatorial election of the municipality where the proposed disposal area is to be located. The petition must be validated by the clerk of the municipality.

(5) The department shall hold the public hearing as described in subsection (4) and all pertinent data received must be evaluated before a construction permit is issued or denied.

(6) The department shall notify the clerk of the municipality in which the disposal area is proposed to be located and the applicant of the department's approval or denial of an application for a construction permit under section 11592 not less than 10 days after the final decision is made.

Sec. 11596. (1) This section applies to coal combustion residuals disposal areas under section 11592.

(2) A person shall not dispose of coal combustion residuals waste at a coal combustion residuals disposal area unless the coal combustion residuals disposal area is licensed under this section. Waste placement in existing units must be consistent with past operating practices or modified practices to ensure good management.

(3) Except as otherwise provided in this section, a person shall not conduct, manage, maintain, or operate a coal combustion residuals disposal area except as authorized by an operating license issued by the department under part 13. The owner or operator of the disposal area shall submit a license application to the department. The application must be made directly to the department. A person authorized by part 115 to operate more than 1 type of coal combustion residuals disposal area at the same facility may apply for a single license.

(4) An applicant for a license for a coal combustion residuals landfill or coal combustion residuals impoundment shall submit evidence of financial assurance that meets the requirements of section 11523 or 11523a, the maximum waste slope in the active portion, an estimate of remaining permitted capacity, and documentation of the amount of waste received at the disposal area during the previous license period or expected to be received, whichever is greater.

(5) An application for a license for a coal combustion residuals disposal area other than an existing coal combustion residuals impoundment must include a certification under the seal of a licensed professional engineer verifying that the construction of the coal combustion residuals disposal area has proceeded according to the approved plans. An applicant for a license for an existing coal combustion residuals impoundment shall submit with the application documentation in the applicant's possession or control regarding the construction of the coal combustion residuals impoundment. If construction of a portion of a coal combustion residuals landfill or coal combustion residuals impoundment is not complete, the owner or operator shall submit additional construction certification of that portion of the coal combustion residuals landfill or coal combustion residuals impoundment under section 11516(3).

(6) An applicant for an operating license, not later than 6 months after a license denial, may resubmit an application with additional information or corrections as are necessary to address the reason for denial, without being required to pay an additional application fee.

(7) An application for an operating license for a coal combustion residuals landfill must be accompanied by a fee of

$13,000.00. By the anniversary of the issuance of the operating license, while the operating license remains in effect, the coal combustion residuals landfill owner or operator shall pay the department a fee of $13,000.00. If the anniversary of the issuance of the operating license falls on a legal holiday, the annual fee must be paid by the next business day.

(8) An application for an operating license for a coal combustion residuals impoundment must be accompanied by a fee of

$13,000.00. By the anniversary of the issuance of the operating license, while the operating license remains in effect, the coal combustion residuals impoundment owner or operator shall pay the department a fee of $13,000.00. If the anniversary of the issuance of the operating license falls on a legal holiday, the annual fee must be paid by the next business day.

(9) The department shall deposit the fees collected under subsections (7) and (8) in the solid waste staff account of the solid waste management fund.

(10) On receipt of a license application for either a coal combustion residuals impoundment or a coal combustion residuals landfill, the department shall do all of the following:

(a) Immediately notify all of the following:

(i) The clerk of the municipality in which the proposed disposal area is located.

(ii) The regional solid waste management planning agency.

(b) Publish a notice in a newspaper or by electronic media with significant circulation or viewership in the vicinity of the proposed disposal area. The notice must contain all of the following:

(i) A map indicating the location of the proposed disposal area.

(ii) A description of the proposed disposal area.

(iii) The location where an individual may review or obtain copies of the complete license application.

(iv) An indication that the department will accept comments for not less than 45 days after the date of publication of the notice.

(v) An indication that the department will hold a public meeting in the area of the disposal area if, not later than 15 days after the date of publication of the notice, any of the following occur:

(A) An applicant or municipality submits a written request for a public meeting to the department.

(B) The department determines that there is a significant public interest in or known public controversy over the application or that for any other reason a public meeting is appropriate.

(11) A public meeting referred to in subsection (10)(b) must be held after the department makes a preliminary review of the application and all pertinent data and before a preliminary decision on the operating license has been made. During the department's review, the department shall consider input provided at the public meeting.

(12) Before issuing a final decision on a license for a coal combustion residuals landfill or coal combustion residuals impoundment, the department shall publish a notice in a newspaper or by electronic media having significant circulation in the vicinity of the proposed disposal area. The notice must contain all of the following:

(a) A map indicating the location of the proposed disposal area.

(b) A description of the proposed disposal area.

(c) A description of the department's draft decision and the location where an individual may review or obtain copies of the draft decision.

(d) An indication that the department will accept comments for 45 days after the date of the notice's publication.

(e) An indication that the department will hold a public meeting in the area of the disposal area if, not later than 15 days after the date of publication of the notice, any of the following occur:

(i) An applicant or a municipality submits a written request for a public meeting to the department.

(ii) The department determines that there is a significant public interest in or known public controversy over the application or that for any other reason a public meeting is appropriate.

(13) If the department returns an application to the applicant as administratively incomplete, the department shall refund the entire application fee. An applicant for a license, not later than 12 months after a license denial or withdrawal of a license application, may resubmit the application with the additional information as needed to address the reasons for denial, without being required to pay an additional application fee.

(14) A person that applies for a license for more than 1 type of coal combustion residuals disposal area at the same facility shall pay a fee equal to the sum of the applicable application fees listed in this section.

(15) The department shall not license a coal combustion residuals landfill or coal combustion residuals impoundment unless the coal combustion residuals landfill or coal combustion residuals impoundment has an approved hydrogeologic monitoring program and the owner or operator has provided the department with the monitoring results. The groundwater monitoring portion of the hydrogeological monitoring program must include sampling and analytical methods that are appropriate for groundwater sampling and that accurately measure hazardous constituents and other monitoring parameters in groundwater samples. Groundwater samples must not be field filtered before laboratory analysis. The department shall use this information in conjunction with other information required by part 115 to determine a course of action regarding the licensing of the facility consistent with section 4005 of subtitle D of the solid waste disposal act, 42 USC 6945, and with part 115. In deciding a course of action, the department shall consider, at a minimum, the environment, natural resources, the public health, safety, and welfare, and other public or private alternatives.

(16) If a coal combustion residuals landfill or coal combustion residuals impoundment violates part 115, the department may do any of the following:

(a) Revoke the coal combustion residuals landfill's or coal combustion residuals impoundment's license.

(b) If the coal combustion residuals disposal area is a coal combustion residuals impoundment that has not been previously licensed under this part, deny a license.

(c) Issue a timetable or schedule of corrective action, including a sequence of actions or operations, that leads to compliance with part 115 within a reasonable time period but not more than 1 year.

(17) The owner or operator of a coal combustion residuals landfill shall annually submit a report to the department and the county and municipality in which the landfill is located that specifies the tonnage and type of coal combustion residuals waste received by the landfill during the year itemized, to the extent possible, by county, state, or country of origin and the amount of remaining disposal capacity at the coal combustion residuals landfill. Remaining disposal capacity must be calculated as the permitted capacity less waste in place for any area that has been constructed and is not yet closed plus the permitted capacity for each area that has a permit for construction under part 115 but has not yet been constructed. The report must be submitted not later than 45 days after the end of each state fiscal year. By January 31 of each year, the department shall submit to the legislature a report summarizing the information obtained under this subsection.
