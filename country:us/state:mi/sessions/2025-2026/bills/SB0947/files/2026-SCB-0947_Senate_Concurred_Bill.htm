Sec. 31503. (1) "Emergency action plan" means a plan developed by the owner that establishes procedures for notification of the department, public off-site authorities, and other agencies of the emergency actions to be taken prior to before, during, and following after an impending or actual failure of a dam.

(2) "Enlargement" means any change in or addition to an existing dam which that raises or may raise the design flood elevation of the water impounded by the dam.

(3) "Failed dam" means a dam not capable of impounding water at its intended level due to a structural deficiency.

(4) "Failure" means an incident resulting in an unplanned or uncontrolled release of water from a dam.

(5) "Federal Energy Regulatory Commission" means the federal agency or a successor federal agency charged with implementing dam safety and licensing requirements for non-federally owned hydropower projects under the federal power act, 16 USC 791a to 825r.

(6) (5) "Flood of record" means the greatest flow rate determined by the department to have occurred at a particular location.

(7) (6) "Freeboard" means the vertical distance between the design flood elevation and the lowest point of the top of the dam.

(8) (7) "Half probable maximum flood" means the largest flood that may reasonably occur over a watershed , and is derived from the combination of hydrologic runoff parameters and the half probable maximum storm precipitation that produces the maximum runoff.

(9) (8) "Half probable maximum storm" precipitation" means the spatial and temporal distribution of the probable maximum precipitation, divided by 2, that produces the maximum volume of precipitation over a watershed.

(10) (9) "Hazard potential classification" means a reference to the potential for loss of life, property damage, and environmental damage in the area downstream of a dam in the event of failure of the dam or appurtenant works.

(11) (10) "Height" means the difference in elevation measured vertically between the natural bed of a stream or watercourse at the downstream toe of the dam, or, if it is not across a stream channel or watercourse, from the lowest elevation of the downstream toe of the dam, to the design flood elevation or to the lowest point of the top of the dam, whichever is less.

(12) (11) "High hazard potential dam" means a dam located in an area where a failure may cause serious damage to inhabited homes, agricultural buildings, campgrounds, recreational facilities, industrial or commercial buildings, public utilities , main highways, or in areas with a population density greater than or equal to 5,000 people per square mile, interstate roads, other freeway roads, other principal arterial roads as defined in the National Functional Classification (NFC) system, class I carrier railroads, or where environmental degradation or natural resource damage would be significant, or where danger to individuals exists with the potential for loss of life.

Sec. 31504. (1) "Impoundment" means the water held back by a dam.

(2) "Inflow design flood" means the flood hydrograph entering the reservoir that is used to design or modify a specific dam and its appurtenant works, including, but not limited to, sizing the spillway and outlet works and evaluating the maximum storage, height of the dam, and freeboard requirements.

(3) (2) "Low hazard potential dam" means a dam located in an area where failure may cause damage limited to agriculture, uninhabited buildings, structures, or township or county minor collector roads, local roads, or nonrated roadways as defined in the National Functional Classification (NFC) system, where environmental degradation would be minimal, and where danger to individuals is slight or nonexistent.

(4) (3) "Maintenance" means the upkeep of a dam and its impoundment and appurtenant works but does not include alterations or repairs.

(5) (4) "One-hundred year flood" means a flood that has a 1% chance of being equaled or exceeded in any given year.

(6) (5) "Owner" means a person who that owns, leases, controls, operates, maintains, manages, or proposes to construct a dam.

(7) "Probable maximum flood" means the largest flood that may reasonably occur over a watershed and is derived from the combination of hydrologic runoff parameters and the probable maximum precipitation that produces the maximum runoff.

(8) (6) "Probable maximum precipitation" means the theoretically greatest depth of precipitation for a given duration that is physically possible over a given size storm area at a particular geographic location at a certain time of year.

Sec. 31505. (1) "Removal" means the physical elimination of a dam or impoundment.

(2) "Repair" means to substantially restore a dam to its original condition and includes only such restoration as that may directly affect the structural integrity of the dam.

(3) "Riparian owner" means a person who that has riparian rights.

(4) "Riparian rights" means rights which that accrue by operation of law to a landowner on the banks of an inland lake or stream.

(5) "Significant hazard potential dam" means a dam located in an area where its failure may cause damage limited to isolated inhabited homes, agricultural buildings, structures, secondary highways, short line or minor arterial roads or major collector roads as defined in the National Functional Classification (NFC) system, railroads not classified as class I, or public utilities in areas with a population density less than 5,000 people per square mile, where environmental degradation may be significant, or where danger to individuals exists.

(6) "Spillway" means a waterway in or about a dam designed for the discharge of water.

(7) "Spillway capacity" means the maximum rate of discharge that will pass through a spillway at design flood elevation.

(8) "Two-hundred year flood" means a flood that has a 0.5% chance of being equaled or exceeded in any given year.

(2) This section does not apply to maintenance performed on a dam that does not affect the structural integrity of the dam.

Sec. 31509a. (1) The department shall establish a staggered registration process that meets all of the following conditions:

(a) Is divided into 5 rounds and each round lasts not more than 2 years, with the first round of registrations beginning 6 months after the effective date of the 2026 amendatory act that added this section.

(b) Registers 1/5 of all dams in this state each round.

(c) Identifies the dams that are subject to registration in each round based on hazard potential classification and other relevant considerations.

(2) Subject to subsection (1), an owner shall register all of its dams with the department on a form and in a manner prescribed by the department. The application for a registration under this section must include all of the following information:

(a) Proof of ownership of the dam.

(b) An asset management plan for all dams owned by the owner, that includes all of the following information:

(i) An inventory of all dam assets.

(ii) An assessment of conditions, remaining life, and critical deficiencies and failure modes, and a life cycle cost analysis.

(iii) An assessment of probability and consequences of dam failure.

(iv) A list of funding sources adequate to cover estimated life cycle costs.

(v) A performance evaluation that includes regulatory compliance, inspection schedule, instrumentation monitoring, and internal operations.

(c) A demonstrated compliance with this part that includes all of the following:

(i) A current inspection report under section 31518 and a plan and schedule approved by the department to address any deficiencies identified in the inspection report, if applicable.

(ii) For high hazard potential and significant hazard potential dams, a current, comprehensive evaluation report under section 31518a, and a plan and schedule approved by the department to address any deficiencies identified in the comprehensive evaluation report, if applicable.

(iii) A current surveillance and monitoring plan under section 31518b.

(iv) A current operation and maintenance plan under section 31518c.

(v) A current safety and security plan under section 31518d.

(d) Any additional information that the department determines is necessary for dam safety.

(3) A registration issued under this section is valid for 10 years. Not later than April 1 of the year the registration expires, to renew a registration, an owner shall submit an application for registration under subsection (2).

(4) The department shall approve or deny an application for a registration not later than 120 days after the application is complete. The department shall deny an application for registration that does not sufficiently demonstrate that the public health, safety, welfare, property, or natural resources, or the public trust in those natural resources, is protected.

(5) The department may revoke a registration, after notice and opportunity for a hearing in accordance with the administrative procedures act of 1969, for any of the following reasons:

(a) A violation of the terms of the registration or a change in the condition of the dam.

(b) The owner failed to disclose relevant facts or otherwise obtained a registration by misrepresentation.

(c) The dam is subject to any of the following:

(i) An order under section 31518(7).

(ii) An order to limit dam operations or an order for the removal of the dam under section 31519.

(iii) An emergency order under section 31521.

(iv) Any other violation under section 31524.

(6) If the department revokes a registration under subsection (5), the department shall notify the owner in writing of the revocation not later than 30 days after the hearing and state the deficiency and request additional information.

(7) If the department denies a registration under subsection (4) or revokes a registration under subsection (5), the owner shall submit a revised application for registration not later than 180 days after the notification is received under subsection (4) or (6).

(8) An owner can transfer a registration to a new owner if both of the following conditions are met:

(a) The new owner is willing and financially capable of complying with the terms of the registration.

(b) The owner receives written approval from the department for the transfer.

(9) The owner shall submit a request for transfer of a registration under subsection (8) on a form and in a manner prescribed by the department. The request for a transfer of a registration must be accompanied by a written agreement between the owner and new owner that states a specific date for the transfer of responsibility.

Sec. 31518. (1) An owner shall submit to the department inspection reports prepared by a licensed professional engineer that evaluate the condition of the dam. The inspection report shall must be submitted as follows:

(a) Not less than once every 3 years year for high hazard potential dams.

(b) Not less than once every 4 2 years for significant hazard potential dams.

(c) Not less than once every 5 years for low hazard potential dams.

(2) The department shall determine the hazard potential classification of for all dams and shall establish an inspection schedule. The department shall notify the owner of each dam's hazard potential classification, the effective date of each hazard potential classification, and each dam's hazard potential history. Not later than 30 days after the owner receives the department's notice of a dam's hazard potential classification, the owner shall provide the department with information regarding how the owner will meet the requirements of the dam's hazard potential classification, including, but not limited to, spillway capacity, emergency action plan, inundation mapping requirements, and an inspection schedule. An inspection schedule shall developed under this subsection must require annual submission of inspection reports for approximately 1/3 of all high hazard potential dams, 1/4 1/2 of all significant hazard potential dams, and 1/5 of all low hazard potential dams. The department shall notify the owners in writing when inspection reports are due. The department may order additional inspection reports following an event or change in condition that could threaten a dam.

(3) An inspection report required by this section shall must include, at a minimum, all of the following:

(a) An evaluation of the dam's condition, spillway capacity, operational adequacy, and structural integrity.

(b) A determination of whether deficiencies exist that could lead to the failure of the dam.

(c) Recommendations for maintenance, repair, and alterations of a dam as are necessary to eliminate any deficiencies.

(d) A schedule to address deficiencies that is approved by the department. An owner violates this part if the owner fails to address deficiencies in accordance with the schedule approved under this subdivision. The department may modify the schedule under this subdivision to address deficiencies based on the conditions of the dam.

(e) An assessment of the appropriateness of the current hazard potential classification established under subsection (1) taking into consideration the current conditions that exist downstream of the dam. An assessment under this subdivision must consider conditions related to damage as identified under a significant hazard potential dam classification and serious damage as identified under a high hazard potential dam classification that might have changed since the effective date of the current hazard potential classification established under subsection (1).

(f) An assessment of operations and maintenance based on the requirements of the current operation and maintenance records prepared under section 31517a.

(g) An evaluation of the risk or changes in risk of significant hazard potential and low hazard potential dams, if applicable.

(h) A review of the original design, construction, maintenance, repair, and probable failure mode records of high hazard potential and significant hazard potential dams, if applicable.

(i) Recommendations for more detailed investigations or evaluations as needed to determine the condition of the dam.

(4) Instead of engaging a licensed professional engineer to prepare an inspection report, local units of government or an organization of the type described in section 31508(2)(a) through (c) 31509(5) may request the department to conduct a visual inspection of a dam owned by that local unit of government and prepare a report on the condition of the dam in accordance with subsection (3). The department shall notify a requesting local unit of government as to when the inspection is to occur.

(5) If an inspection report discloses the need for a more detailed investigation or evaluation of certain dam features for the purpose of determining the condition of the dam, the department may order the completion and submission of that detailed investigation or evaluation at the expense of the owner. An investigation or evaluation required under this subsection shall must be conducted under the supervision of a licensed professional engineer.

(6) If an owner does not submit an inspection report as required by subsection (1) or conduct additional investigations if required by subsection (5), the department or any person who that would have life or property threatened by a breach of the dam may have a report prepared and recover the costs of preparing the report in a civil action. commenced in a court of competent jurisdiction. This subsection does not limit the right of any person to bring a cause of action in a court of proper jurisdiction to compel an owner to comply with the requirements of this part.

(7) If, based on the findings and recommendations of the inspection report, and an inspection by the department, or if the department finds that a condition exists which that endangers a the dam, it the department shall order the owner to take actions that the department considers necessary to alleviate the danger.

Sec. 31518a. (1) For high hazard potential and significant hazard potential dams, an owner shall submit to the department a comprehensive evaluation report prepared by a licensed professional engineer that evaluates the overall safety of the dam. For low hazard potential dams, the department may require an owner to submit a comprehensive evaluation report as provided under this subsection. The comprehensive evaluation report must be submitted not less than once every 10 years on a schedule determined by the department.

(2) The comprehensive evaluation report must include, but is not limited to, a detailed evaluation and recommendation for all of the following:

(a) The dam's condition, spillway capacity, operational adequacy, and structural integrity.

(b) The adequacy of the dam's original design and construction.

(c) The adequacy of the dam's operation and maintenance procedures.

(d) Probable failure modes and risk reduction measures.

(e) Conformance with current dam safety practices.

(f) Any deficiencies that exist that could lead to failure of the dam.

(g) Maintenance, repair, and alterations of a dam that are necessary to eliminate the deficiencies described under subdivision (f).
